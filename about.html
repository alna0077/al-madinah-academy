About Al-Madinah

Learning rooted in the Qur’an and authentic Sunnah.

Al-Madinah is an Ottawa academy for children, families, sisters, and adult learners seeking structured Qur’an, Arabic, and Islamic Studies education in a calm, respectful environment.

Mission & approach

Strong foundations, taught with clarity and care.

Our aim is to connect students to the Qur’an and Sunnah through careful correction, clear teaching, and knowledge that informs practice.

Qur’an study develops correct recitation, Tajweed, and steady confidence. Arabic opens a pathway toward reading and understanding. Islamic Studies builds sound foundations in belief, worship, manners, and daily conduct. Across every subject, students progress through assessment, guided practice, review, and patient feedback.

Adab is part of the learning itself: students are encouraged to listen attentively, ask respectfully, practise consistently, and carry what they learn into everyday life.

Educational framework

Qur’an · Sunnah · Knowledge · Adab

Correct with patience. Accuracy grows through attentive teaching and repeatable practice.

Progress with structure. Each next step is grounded in what the student can already do.

Connect knowledge to practice. Learning should shape worship, character, and conduct.

Leadership & educators

Teaching leadership and academy direction, working together.

Al-Madinah’s educational work is guided by two distinct, complementary areas of responsibility.

Ustadhah Ayah leads Qur’an and Islamic Studies teaching with deep experience in recitation, Qira’at, memorization, and patient correction. Ustadh Emad guides the academy’s vision, structure, operations, educational systems, and community work.

01

Teaching leadership

Co-Founder & Lead Qur’an & Islamic Studies Educator

Ustadhah Ayah

More than 25 years of Qur’an education, with a focus on accurate recitation, Qira’at, memorization, patient correction, and sound Islamic learning.

Ustadhah Ayah memorized the Qur’an at an early age and later studied at Imam Muhammad ibn Saud Islamic University, completing a program focused on preparing Qur’an and Islamic Studies educators.

She has received ijazat in Qira’at, including Hafs and Shu‘bah from ‘Aasim and Qalun from Nafi‘. She teaches these recitations and grants ijazah to qualified students after successful completion of the required recitation and study.

Her Islamic Studies background includes Aqeedah, Tawheed, Fiqh, Hadith, and documented study of classical texts. A native Arabic speaker fluent in English, she supports Arabic-speaking and non-Arabic-speaking students across ages and levels.

Her Ottawa experience includes coordinating Qur’an programs at local masajid, developing classes and curriculum, communicating with families, and overseeing a girls’ program serving approximately 80–100 students.

Sisters’ learning initiative

Rooted in Ottawa. Teaching beyond borders.

Led by Ustadhah Ayah, this dedicated initiative supports sisters and girls in a calm, comfortable Islamic learning environment.

Teaching may include Qur’an, Qira’at where appropriate, Arabic support, and Islamic Studies. Alongside learners in Ottawa, online study currently reaches students in Halifax and the United States—modest evidence that careful teaching can remain personal across distance.

Online participation is arranged according to student level, program fit, and teacher availability.

02

Academy direction

Co-Founder & Academy Director

Ustadh Emad Alnaji

More than two decades of professional, educational, and community experience applied to the academy’s vision, structure, operations, and long-term development.

A graduate of King Saud University in Riyadh, Ustadh Emad spent formative years living and studying in an Arabic-speaking environment shaped by Islamic learning and culture. He has continued studies in Aqeedah, Tawheed, Fiqh, Hadith, Seerah, Tajweed, Arabic, Nahw, and documented classical texts.

As a native Arabic speaker fluent in English, he supports Arabic-speaking and non-Arabic-speaking learners with clarity.

In Ottawa, his community work has included developing Qur’an halaqas and Islamic educational programs, curriculum selection, class organization, family communication, instructor coordination, and service as a local masjid board member.

His background in project management, technology, and digital systems supports the academy’s educational platforms, program delivery, operations, and future initiatives.
